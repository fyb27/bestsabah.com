Island Hopping from KK: The Honest Guide to Tunku Abdul Rahman Park

June 2026 · 8 min read · Things To Do

Everyone who comes to KK wants to do the islands. And honestly | they should. Tunku Abdul Rahman Park sits right off the coast, five islands you can reach in a 15-minute boat ride from Jesselton Point. The water is genuinely that blue. The fish are genuinely that many.

But here's what the travel blogs don't tell you: not all five islands are worth your time, the boat operators at the jetty will absolutely oversell you, and going on a weekend in peak season is a different experience from going on a quiet Tuesday morning.

I've done this trip more times than I can count | sometimes with friends visiting from KL, sometimes just to clear my head. Here's the real breakdown.

The Five Islands

Pulau Sapi is the most accessible and the most crowded. Closest to the mainland, easiest boat, most facilities. Go here if you want a chill beach day with a proper changing room and food stalls nearby. Don't go here if you want quiet | on weekends it's packed.

Pulau Manukan is the biggest and probably the best overall. Long beach, decent snorkelling on the eastern end, a small resort if you want to overnight it. The water here is consistently cleaner than Sapi. This is my default recommendation for first-timers.

Pulau Mamutik is small and no-frills. Less visited, which means the reef is in better shape. If you actually care about snorkelling | fish variety, coral, visibility | go here. Bring your own food and water because the facilities are minimal.

Pulau Sulug is the furthest out and rarely visited. Quiet, basic, and genuinely peaceful. You'll need to arrange the boat separately | most operators don't go there by default. Worth it if you want to feel like you have an island to yourself.

Pulau Gaya is the largest but the least touristy of the five. There's a water village on one end and a resort on the other. Most day-trippers skip it. Honestly, unless you're staying at the resort or want to see the water village, it's not a typical island-hop stop.

How to Get There

Jesselton Point Ferry Terminal is the main departure point. It's right in the city centre, walking distance from most hotels. The terminal opens around 7:30am and boats run until mid-afternoon | don't cut it too close to 3pm if you want a comfortable return.

You'll buy your boat ticket at the counters outside. The standard package is a return boat to 2-3 islands, plus a marine park entry fee (RM10 per person for Malaysians, RM30 for foreigners). Boat prices vary | shop around because different operators quote different prices, and everything is negotiable, especially for groups.

When to Go

Weekday mornings. Full stop. If you can go Tuesday to Thursday, before 10am, you'll have a completely different experience from a Saturday afternoon. The water is the same colour but the crowd is a fraction of the size.

March to October is generally the best weather window. November through February can be rough seas | boats sometimes don't run on bad weather days, so check before you go if you're visiting during that window.

What to Bring

Snorkel gear | you can rent at the islands but owning your own makes a difference in fit and cleanliness. Reef-safe sunscreen (the regular stuff damages coral and the operators are increasingly strict about it). Cash | most island food stalls don't take cards. A dry bag if you have one.

The Bottom Line

Do it. Even if you've seen beaches before, KK's islands are genuinely beautiful and close enough to the city that there's no reason not to. Go to Manukan for a balanced day, Mamutik if you're serious about snorkelling, Sapi if you want the easiest option.

Just go on a weekday. You'll thank yourself.
