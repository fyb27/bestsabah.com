Island Hopping from KK: The Honest Guide to Tunku Abdul Rahman Park

Things To Do 8 min read

Five islands. One park. Which ones are worth it, which are overrated, and when to go to beat the crowds.

Tunku Abdul Rahman Park from Jesselton Point jetty

Everyone who comes to KK wants to do the islands. And honestly, they should. Tunku Abdul Rahman Park sits right off the coast, five islands you can reach in a 15-minute boat ride from Jesselton Point. The water is genuinely that blue. The fish are genuinely that many.

But here's what the travel blogs don't tell you: not all five islands are worth your time, the boat operators at the jetty will absolutely oversell you, and going on a weekend in peak season is a completely different experience from going on a quiet Tuesday morning.

I've done this trip more times than I can count. Here's the real breakdown.

The Five Islands

Pulau Sapi is the most accessible and the most crowded. Closest to the mainland, easiest boat, most facilities. Go here if you want a chill beach day with a proper changing room and food stalls nearby. Don't go here if you want quiet. On weekends it's packed.

Pulau Manukan is the biggest and probably the best overall. Long beach, decent snorkelling on the eastern end, a small resort if you want to overnight it. This is my default recommendation for first-timers.

The water at Manukan on a good day. This is what you're coming for.

Pulau Mamutik is small and no-frills. Less visited, which means the reef is in better shape. If you actually care about snorkelling, go here. Bring your own food and water.

Pulau Sulug is the furthest out and rarely visited. Quiet, basic, genuinely peaceful. Most operators don't go there by default. Worth it if you want to feel like you have an island to yourself.

Pulau Gaya is the largest but least touristy. There's a water village on one end and a resort on the other. Most day-trippers skip it.

How to Get There

Jesselton Point. Get here before 9am.

Jesselton Point Ferry Terminal is the main departure point, right in the city centre. The terminal opens around 7:30am and boats run until mid-afternoon. Don't cut it too close to 3pm if you want a comfortable return.

The standard package is a return boat to 2-3 islands plus a marine park entry fee (RM10 for Malaysians, RM30 for foreigners). Shop around because prices vary and everything is negotiable for groups.

When to Go

Weekday mornings. Full stop. Go Tuesday to Thursday, before 10am. The water is the same colour but the crowd is a fraction of the size.

March to October is the best weather window. November through February can bring rough seas. Check before you go.

What to Bring

Mamutik has the best reef. Bring your own mask.

Snorkel gear, reef-safe sunscreen, cash (most island stalls don't take cards), and a dry bag. That's the full list.

The Bottom Line

Do it. Go to Manukan for a balanced day, Mamutik if you're serious about snorkelling, Sapi if you want the easiest option. Just go on a weekday. You'll thank yourself.
