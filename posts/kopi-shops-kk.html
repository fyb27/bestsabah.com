KK's Best Kopi Shops: Old Corners, Strong Brews, and Good Mornings

Cafes 5 min read

The traditional kopi culture in Kota Kinabalu is alive, unhurried, and quietly wonderful. Here's where to find it.

There's a version of breakfast in KK that hasn't changed much in fifty years. You sit at a marble-top table on a plastic chair. A glass of kopi-o arrives, dark and sweet and slightly bitter in the back of your throat. There's kaya toast, or maybe a bowl of curry mee if you're hungry. The ceiling fans turn slowly. Nobody is in a hurry.

This is the kopi shop. And KK still has plenty of them if you know where to look.

What Makes a Proper Kopi Shop

Not all kopi shops are equal. A proper one has been around for at least a generation, uses its own roasted beans (often in-house), serves drinks in ceramic cups or thick glass, and has a crowd of regulars who arrive at the same time every morning and sit in the same seats.

The drinks themselves are simple: kopi (coffee with condensed milk), kopi-o (black with sugar), kopi-c (with evaporated milk), and the same variations for teh (tea). You can get them hot or iced. The iced versions, pulled from a height to create froth, are genuinely good.

Where to Go

The old shophouses around Gaya Street are the most reliable hunting ground. Sunday Market aside, Gaya Street on a regular weekday morning has several kopi shops that have been running for decades. Look for the ones with the old signboards, the ones that open before 7am, and the ones where you can hear the sound of cups being washed in the back.

Around the Central Market area you'll find kopi shops that serve the market traders and early morning workers. These are the purists. Plain tables, no air-conditioning, sometimes no menu because the regulars never need one. Point at what the person next to you is drinking if you're not sure.

The Penampang road stretch heading out of the city has a few old-school kopitiam that the newer generation has mostly forgotten. Worth the detour if you have a car.

What to Order

First visit: kopi-c ais (iced coffee with evaporated milk) and kaya toast with soft-boiled eggs. That's the classic combination and it's classic for a reason.

If you want to try something more local, ask for teh tarik or, if they have it, kopi kampung, which is a coarser, earthier brew made from locally grown beans. Not every shop has it but when they do it's worth ordering.

The Honest Take

The specialty coffee wave has hit KK and there are good third-wave cafes in the city now. Nothing against them. But the kopi shop experience is something else entirely. It's slower. It's more honest. It's coffee as a social ritual rather than a product.

If you're visiting KK, spend at least one morning in a proper kopi shop. Order the same thing twice. Talk to whoever sits down next to you. That's the point of the place.
