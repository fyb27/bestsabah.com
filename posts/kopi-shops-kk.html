KK's Best Kopi Shops: Old Corners, Strong Brews, and Good Mornings

Best Food 5 min read

The traditional kopi culture in Kota Kinabalu is alive, unhurried, and quietly wonderful. Here's where to find it.

The kind of place where regulars sit in the same seat every morning

There's a version of breakfast in KK that hasn't changed much in fifty years. You sit at a marble-top table on a plastic chair. A glass of kopi-o arrives, dark and sweet and slightly bitter. There's kaya toast, or maybe a bowl of curry mee. The ceiling fans turn slowly. Nobody is in a hurry.

This is the kopi shop. And KK still has plenty of them if you know where to look.

What Makes a Proper Kopi Shop

A proper one has been around for at least a generation, uses its own roasted beans, serves drinks in ceramic cups or thick glass, and has regulars who sit in the same seats every morning.

Kopi-c ais. Order this first.

The drinks are simple: kopi (with condensed milk), kopi-o (black with sugar), kopi-c (with evaporated milk). Same variations for teh. Hot or iced. The iced versions, pulled from a height to create froth, are genuinely good.

Where to Go

Gaya Street is the most reliable hunting ground. Several kopi shops here have been running for decades. Look for the ones that open before 7am and where you can hear cups being washed in the back.

Gaya Street on a weekday morning. Before the Sunday Market crowd, this is the real KK.

Around the Central Market you'll find kopi shops serving the market traders and early morning workers. Plain tables, no air-conditioning, sometimes no menu. Point at what the person next to you is drinking.

The Penampang road stretch has a few old-school kopitiam the newer generation has mostly forgotten. Worth the detour if you have a car.

What to Order

Kaya toast with soft-boiled eggs. The combination is non-negotiable.

First visit: kopi-c ais and kaya toast with soft-boiled eggs. The eggs come in a small bowl, slightly runny, with a dash of soy sauce and white pepper. Dip the toast. Don't overthink it.

If you want something more local, ask for kopi kampung, a coarser earthier brew from locally grown beans. Not every shop has it but when they do, order it.

The Honest Take

The specialty coffee wave has hit KK and there are good third-wave cafes now. Nothing against them. But the kopi shop experience is something else entirely. Slower. More honest. Coffee as a social ritual rather than a product.

Spend at least one morning in a proper kopi shop. Order the same thing twice. That's the point of the place.
