Kundasang in the Morning: Sabah's Highland Secret Two Hours from KK

Hidden Gems 6 min read

Cool air, vegetable farms, and one of the best views in all of Borneo. Most KK locals have been. Most tourists haven't heard of it.

The kind of view that makes a 5am drive worth it

You leave KK before dawn. The highway is quiet, the air through the window already smells different by the time you pass Tambunan. Two hours later you're in Kundasang, and it's cold enough that you actually want a jacket. In Sabah. In June.

That's the first thing that gets you. The temperature. KK sits at sea level and runs hot. Kundasang is at about 1,500 metres, and on a clear morning you'll be standing in the kind of air you normally only find in Cameron Highlands or Genting. Except without the crowds, the traffic, or the strawberry stalls every five metres.

What's Actually There

Kundasang is a small town at the base of Mount Kinabalu, Sabah's biggest mountain and one of the highest peaks in Southeast Asia. The town itself is unremarkable. What isn't unremarkable is everything surrounding it.

The Kundasang vegetable farms. Rows of cabbage and corn running up the hillside.

The vegetable farms are the first thing people notice. The cool highland climate means Kundasang produces most of Sabah's vegetables: cabbages, corn, tomatoes, leafy greens. The fields run up the hillsides in neat rows and in the morning light they look almost European. It's genuinely beautiful and completely unexpected.

The Kundasang War Memorial sits quietly on a hill overlooking the valley. It honours the soldiers who died on the Sandakan Death March during World War II. It's not a loud monument. Just well-kept gardens, some plaques, and a view that makes you stand still for a while. Worth an hour of your time.

Mount Kinabalu on a clear morning. By 10am the clouds usually roll in. Be early.

And then there's the view of Mount Kinabalu itself. On a clear morning, from the right spot on the road just outside town, you get an unobstructed view of the summit. The mountain is enormous in a way that photos don't capture. It fills a third of the sky.

Where to Eat

There are a handful of small restaurants along the main road serving simple Malay and local food. Don't overthink it. Order the corn soup if it's on the menu. The corn comes from right there and it tastes like it.

Most people stop at one of the roadside stalls selling fresh vegetables to take back to KK. Prices are noticeably cheaper than the city and the produce is fresher. Bring a cooler box if you're serious about it.

When to Go

Leave KK by 5:30 or 6am if you want the morning light and a clear view of the mountain before clouds roll in. By 10am the summit is usually covered. That's just how it works up there.

Most people do Kundasang as a day trip. That's enough. Two hours up, a few hours exploring, lunch, two hours back. You're home by 3pm.

Getting There

Drive. There's no comfortable public transport option that makes sense for a day trip. Rent a car in KK, take the Tambunan road, and follow the signs toward Ranau. Kundasang is on that route, you can't miss it.

The road is good. Some stretches are winding but nothing dramatic. Just don't do it tired at night.

The Honest Take

Kundasang won't change your life. It's not that kind of place. But it's the kind of place that reminds you why Sabah is genuinely different from everywhere else in Malaysia. You can go from sea level to cool highland in two hours. You can stand next to a vegetable farm, look up, and see one of the great mountains of Southeast Asia filling the sky above you.

That's worth getting up early for.
