Living and Working Remotely in KK: An Honest One-Year Review

Lifestyle 9 min read

I've been working from home in Kota Kinabalu for over a year. Here's what the digital nomad blogs don't tell you, and what they get right.

Every few months a new article appears somewhere ranking KK as one of Southeast Asia's best cities for remote workers. The articles are usually written by someone who spent two weeks here, found a nice cafe with good wifi, and left before the novelty wore off.

I've lived here my whole life and worked remotely for the past year and a half. Here's a more honest version.

KK from Signal Hill. The view you get to see whenever you want when you live here.

The Good Stuff (It's Real)

Cost of living is genuinely low. A comfortable one-bedroom apartment in a decent area runs between RM800 and RM1,500 a month. Food, even eating out daily, is unlikely to cost you more than RM600-800 a month unless you're eating at hotel restaurants every night. For someone earning in USD or euros, this city is very affordable.

Several cafes in KK are implicitly remote-worker friendly. Power points, good wifi, no rush.

The internet situation has improved significantly. Fibre is widely available in most parts of the city now. Unifi and TIME are the two most reliable providers. Getting speeds of 100-500Mbps is straightforward. The days of terrible Malaysian internet are mostly over, at least in KK.

The quality of life outside work is high. You can be on an island in 20 minutes. You can drive to a waterfall in 45 minutes. The food is good, cheap, and available at all hours. The pace is slower than KL in a way that most people find either relaxing or boring depending on their personality.

The Less Good Stuff (Also Real)

There is no tech or startup ecosystem here. If you need to be around other people doing similar work, doing spontaneous coffee meetings with potential collaborators, or attending industry events, KK will frustrate you. The professional community here is small and largely offline.

Public transport is essentially non-existent for practical daily use. You need a car or you need to Grab everywhere. Grab works fine in the city but coverage drops off quickly outside the main areas.

On the island in 20 minutes. This is the upside that the digital nomad blogs get right.

The heat is real. KK is hot every single day. If you're from a temperate climate you'll adjust, but you'll be adjusting for longer than you think. Air-conditioning is not optional.

Getting in and out requires a flight. There's no train to KL, no bus that makes practical sense. A flight to KL takes 2.5 hours and costs RM150-400 depending on when you book. For most people this is fine. But if you need to be mobile frequently, factor in the time and cost.

Who KK WFH Works For

It works really well if you're already here, already have your social network established, and just want to work remotely rather than go into an office. That's my situation and it's good.

It works reasonably well if you're a solo remote worker who values low costs, nature access, and a quiet life over professional networking and urban energy.

It works less well if you're a digital nomad who needs community, spontaneity, and a constant stream of new people. Bali and Chiang Mai serve that need better.

The Honest Bottom Line

KK is a genuinely good city to live in. It's not a great nomad hub. Those are different things. If the distinction matters to you, think carefully before committing to a long stay.

If it doesn't matter to you, and you just want somewhere affordable, beautiful, and low-stress to do your work and live your life, KK delivers on that consistently.
