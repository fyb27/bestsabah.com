Living and Working Remotely in KK: An Honest One-Year Review

Lifestyle 9 min read

I've been working from home in Kota Kinabalu for over a year. Here's what the digital nomad blogs don't tell you, and what they get right.

KK from Signal Hill. The view you get whenever you want when you live here.

Every few months a new article appears somewhere ranking KK as one of Southeast Asia's best cities for remote workers. The articles are usually written by someone who spent two weeks here, found a nice cafe with good wifi, and left before the novelty wore off.

I've lived here my whole life and worked remotely for the past year and a half. Here's a more honest version.

The Good Stuff (It's Real)

Cost of living is genuinely low. A comfortable one-bedroom apartment in a decent area runs between RM800 and RM1,500 a month. Food, even eating out daily, is unlikely to cost more than RM600-800 a month. For someone earning in USD or euros, this city is very affordable.

Several cafes in KK are implicitly remote-worker friendly.

The internet has improved significantly. Fibre is widely available. Unifi and TIME are the two most reliable providers. Getting 100-500Mbps is straightforward. The days of terrible Malaysian internet are mostly over, at least in KK.

The quality of life outside work is high. On an island in 20 minutes. At a waterfall in 45 minutes. Food is good, cheap, and available at all hours.

The Less Good Stuff (Also Real)

There is no tech or startup ecosystem here. If you need to be around other people doing similar work, or attending industry events, KK will frustrate you. The professional community is small and largely offline.

Public transport is essentially non-existent for practical daily use. You need a car or Grab everywhere. Grab works fine in the city but coverage drops off quickly outside the main areas.

On the island in 20 minutes. This is the upside the digital nomad blogs get right.

The heat is real. KK is hot every single day. Air-conditioning is not optional. Getting in and out requires a flight. A flight to KL takes 2.5 hours and costs RM150-400 depending on when you book.

Coworking Spaces

There are a handful in the city. Quality varies. The genuine coworking experience, community events and collaboration, is limited. Most remote workers end up working from home, cafes, or a combination of both. The cafe option is actually good.

Who KK WFH Works For

It works really well if you're already here, already have your social network, and just want to work remotely rather than go into an office. That's my situation and it's good.

It works reasonably well for solo remote workers who value low costs, nature access, and a quiet life over professional networking.

It works less well if you're a nomad who needs community and a constant stream of new people. Bali and Chiang Mai serve that need better.

The Honest Bottom Line

KK is a genuinely good city to live in. It's not a great nomad hub. Those are different things. If you just want somewhere affordable, beautiful, and low-stress to do your work and live your life, KK delivers on that consistently.
